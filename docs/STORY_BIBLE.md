## 1. Premise
THE LAST FRAME is a psychological mystery about time, space, memory, identity, loss, and what wanting to change the past costs.

Elias wakes in a long-abandoned station. He doesn't remember who he is, how he got there, or why the whole world seems to have stopped at **23:59**. All he has is a mysterious **pocket watch**.

He gradually learns that the world he is in is not fully real. The city, its buildings, its people and its events are tied to an experiment called **ELYSIUM**. Behind it all stands **the Operator**, who knows too much about Elias and sometimes seems to have already witnessed journeys Elias hasn't made yet.

**Who the Operator is, the game never answers explicitly.** Players build their own theories from the clues.

## 2. Themes and central question
- **Time:** can the past really be changed?
- **Space:** is the space we stand in real?
- **Memory:** does remembering prove something happened?
- **Identity:** is someone still themselves after countless loops?
- **Loss:** is holding on to someone more important than letting them go?
- **Causality:** do we create the future, or repeat what already happened?

**Central question, never answered by the narration:** *"Are we repeating the past, or are we creating it?"*

**Guiding line:** *"Somewhere between what was and what could have been, we lost ourselves."*

## 3. World
- **Year 2097, Elysium City:** a centre of research into temporal technology and memory reconstruction.
- **The ELYSIUM project** was built to:
  1. record traces of past events
  2. store patterns of human consciousness and memory
  3. reconstruct events from temporal data
  4. observe possible changes in a sequence of time
  5. allow limited interaction between temporal states
- It started as a scientific and humanitarian project. Under some conditions it **creates causal links between states of time**. Its last experiment started the disaster that froze the city at 23:59.
- **The frozen city:**
  - Rain hangs in the air, trains stop between stations, and lights flicker without ever going out.
  - Some people remain only as recordings or temporal shadows. In Ch1 these are the frozen commuters, grey "statues" whose last minutes Trace can read.
  - Not every place is in the same state. Some rooms show the past, some a possible future, some a mix. The city is a temporal labyrinth of what ELYSIUM left behind.
- **Station Elysium-09 (Ch1):** an underground transit station. It once served the line to the main ELYSIUM research facility ("A1 → Research", "R&D Access"; reference `design/art/reference/ch01/`). It was evacuated at 23:54, and every clock stopped at 23:59.
